# Priority Inbox research for T3 Code

2026-11-21 · First research pass

## The problem

One engineer can start several agents across active projects and receive results faster than they can review them. Each return to T3 Code can require two decisions: which thread deserves attention, and what they were trying to achieve when they last worked on it. A completed turn could be a diff to review, an answer to read, or a status update that needs nothing from the engineer. Meanwhile, a stopped agent may be waiting on a decision that unblocks more work than the newest completion.

The closest product comparisons address separate parts of this. VS Code's experimental Agents Inbox lets the engineer acknowledge current activity and resurface a session upon new activity. Sunsama connects weekly outcomes to tasks, and its Agent Threads beta can attach several agent conversations to one task. Momentum puts one chosen goal in the path of a routine return. These products establish that the interactions are possible. Whether they help a solo T3 Code user catch the right handoffs across projects remains open. [VS Code Agents Inbox](https://code.visualstudio.com/docs/agents/run/sessions/organize-sessions) · [Sunsama Weekly Objectives](https://help.sunsama.com/docs/usage-guides/weekly-objectives/) · [Sunsama Agent Threads](https://help.sunsama.com/docs/ai/agent-threads/) · [Momentum Daily Goal](https://get.momentumdash.help/hc/en-us/articles/360012256274-Daily-Goal)

## What the research says

| Finding | Implication for AI coding threads |
| --- | --- |
| **An inbox doubles as a reminder system.** Whittaker and Sidner observed people using email for communication, filing, and task management. Leaving messages visible preserved reminders, while accumulation made the inbox harder to use. Bellotti and colleagues found that email users had workable priority judgments and spent effort maintaining the reminders and queues that supported them. [Whittaker and Sidner, CHI 1996](https://students.lti.cs.cmu.edu/11899/files/cp3a-whittaker-sidner.pdf) · [Bellotti et al., CHI 2004](https://doi.org/10.1145/985692.985785) | A small attention queue needs a way to find deferred and older items. A star can preserve a reference after the latest update has been handled. |
| **Urgency can crowd out importance.** In controlled studies, people sometimes chose lower-value tasks when they appeared to have shorter completion windows. Gmail's original system used the predicted likelihood of action as a proxy for importance. [Zhu, Yang, and Hsee, 2018](https://academic.oup.com/jcr/article-abstract/45/3/673/4847790) · [Google's Priority Inbox paper, 2010](https://research.google/pubs/the-learning-behind-gmail-priority-inbox/) | A real dependency or deadline can justify urgency. Recency and likelihood of clicking mostly tell us which item is salient. Fast responses are weak evidence that the right project moved forward. |
| **Checking has a cost of its own.** In a two-week field experiment with 124 adults, participants reported less daily stress during the week they were asked to limit email checks. The experiment measured stress. It did not measure how reliably participants caught time-critical work. [Kushlev and Dunn, 2015](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.chb.2014.11.005) | Batching ordinary completions is a distinct approach from reordering a live feed. A waiting approval or other time-sensitive handoff can still warrant an interruption. |
| **Resuming work costs context.** Leroy's experiments found that thoughts about unfinished work interfere with the next task. Leroy and Glomb found that preparing a brief plan for resumption reduced that effect in their studies. Parnin and Rugaber analyzed logs from 85 programmers. In 7% of sessions, developers made an edit without first navigating to other locations. [Leroy, 2009](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0749597809000399) · [Leroy and Glomb, 2018](https://doi.org/10.1287/orsc.2017.1184) · [Parnin and Rugaber, 2009](https://chrisparnin.me/pdf/parnin-icpc09.pdf) | The list may get the engineer to the right thread. The next action, relevant diff, or a short note about where they left off may determine whether they can act on it. |
| **A specific plan can ease the mental load of unfinished goals.** Masicampo and Baumeister found that making a plan for an unfulfilled goal reduced its interference with other tasks in their experiments. [Masicampo and Baumeister, 2011](https://users.wfu.edu/masicaej/MasicampoBaumeister2011JPSP.pdf) | A weekly outcome may be easier to resume when paired with a concrete next step or thread. A passive list and using that list to rank agent outputs are separate hypotheses. |
| **Parallel agents add monitoring and review.** A 17-interview preprint describes oversight before, during, and after agent work. ParallelPilot's preprint reports higher ticket throughput and lower perceived tracking and switching burden in a short, 16-person within-subject study of a bundled interface. Verification accuracy was outside that study. [Dhanorkar et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.05391) · [ParallelPilot](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.33113) | “Turn ended,” “I saw it,” “I handled it,” and “the work is accepted” need independent meanings. Shorter triage is one benefit. Judging the result still takes engineering attention. |

The email and interruption studies explain costs that could transfer to this product. The coding studies offer closer evidence, with limits: the log study predates coding agents, the oversight interviews are concentrated in one organization, and ParallelPilot evaluates several features together. This pass uses published papers, preprints, official product docs, and public screenshots. It contains no T3 Code user interviews or usage data. The [product precedents](product-precedents.md) document what each comparison actually does.

The [follow-up on finishing work](finishing-work.md) covers task juggling, work in progress, and the review effort that continues after an engineer opens a PR.

## What T3 Code already does

| Surface | Current behavior |
| --- | --- |
| **Active and Working** | The Working beta folds ongoing work out of the active list. A completion, failure, approval request, or request for an answer can bring a thread back. Active is ordered by when each thread last came back to the user. Pins stay above Working. Web and desktop also have a beta project-grouped sidebar. [Working](../../docs/user/thread-sidebar.md#fold-working-threads-beta) · [Group by project](../../docs/user/thread-sidebar.md#group-threads-by-project-beta) |
| **Pin and Settle** | Pin holds a thread at the top. Settle moves it out of Active, removes its pin, and can dismiss pending async questions or trigger configured terminal actions. Opening a thread and settling it already serve different purposes. [Thread sidebar guide](../../docs/user/thread-sidebar.md#settle-finished-work) |
| **Project selection** | Web and desktop already have a searchable project picker and a persistent multi-project selection with Shift-click or Shift-Enter. Native Home lets the user pick an environment and one project. Discoverability and parity are concrete issues to investigate. [Web sidebar](../../apps/web/src/components/Sidebar.tsx) · [Native Home](../../apps/mobile/src/features/home/HomeRouteScreen.tsx) |
| **Visits and freshness** | The visit watermark tracks which thread content the engineer has seen. It can use the thread's last update or wake time. The stored value cannot tell us when they navigated away. [Shared visit tracking](../../packages/client-runtime/src/state/threadVisits.ts) |
| **No thread open** | On web and desktop, `/` starts a draft in a recent eligible project when one exists. Native expanded mode has a “Select a thread” empty detail pane. A recurring home would need a deliberate entry point on each client. [Web entry route](../../apps/web/src/routes/_chat.index.tsx) · [Native empty detail](../../apps/mobile/src/features/layout/WorkspaceEmptyDetail.tsx) |

## Questions the evidence leaves open

- Which events actually deserve one engineer's attention across projects? A blocked agent, a result awaiting review, a failed check after a result, and a completion after the engineer has moved on are different cases.
- When an engineer says “when I left the thread,” do they want the last time they navigated away, when the agent handed work back, or whether both events occurred in a particular order? Each answers a different return-to-work question.
- What makes a visible list feel bounded without causing older work to disappear from attention? Existing products provide precedent for sections, overflow, and saved queues. They provide no T3 Code-specific row limit.
- Would people maintain a weekly intent list? Would they value it on the home screen in a quiet week? Would matching against it surface more useful handoffs than project, state, and timing alone?
